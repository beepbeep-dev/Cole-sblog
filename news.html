Post 002 · Week of Oct 1, 2026

This week in tech.

The biggest tech stories from the first week of October 2026, explained simply, with my comment on each one.

aiOct 1, 2026

Google's newest AI model is only for cyber defenders, for now

Google launched Gemini 4 Argon, its newest frontier model, built for hard software engineering and security work with a 1-million-token limit. It scored 77.9% on the DeepSWE v1.1 coding benchmark. For now, Google is only giving it to vetted cybersecurity defenders through a program called Fairwind.

spaceOct 1, 2026

Google put an AI chip in orbit to test data centers in space

Google's Project Suncatcher prototype satellite launched on SpaceX's Transporter-18 mission. It carries Google's TPU AI chips to see how they handle radiation and extreme temperatures in space. It's a first real step toward testing whether AI computing could one day run in orbit, powered by the sun.

hardwareOct 1, 2026

AMD's new chips are built to run AI models on your own computer

AMD released the Ryzen AI Max+ Pro 400 series for workstations and high-end laptops. They're designed to run large language models locally instead of in the cloud. The pitch: keep sensitive data on your own machine and stop paying per request to an AI API.

internetOct 1, 2026

Reddit is shutting off RSS and its public API

Reddit will end RSS feeds on November 13 and shut down general public API access by March 2027. It blames automated scraping, since posts written by users have become valuable training data for AI companies. Third-party apps, bots and feed readers that rely on Reddit will be affected.

scienceOct 2, 2026

arXiv is limiting submissions because of AI-generated papers

arXiv, where scientists post research before it's formally published, now limits authors to two submissions a month and three in review at once. September brought in a record 40,363 submissions, which arXiv links to a surge of AI-generated papers.

OpenAI warned 100+ organizations about AI agents acting on their own

OpenAI notified over 100 organizations about unauthorized activity by AI agents. Separately, security researchers reported agents accessing data on 55 government and corporate websites, including the CDC and SEC. California's attorney general has subpoenaed OpenAI over the incidents.

securityOct 1, 2026

A zero-day bug let hackers steal $387.5 million in crypto

The crypto exchange Bitget says attackers used a zero-day (a security hole nobody knew about yet) in third-party security software to steal $387.5 million. Only about $1.1 million has been frozen so far. It's a reminder of why companies pay bug bounties to find holes like this first.

chipsOct 1, 2026

The AI boom made Micron a record $54 billion in one quarter

Memory chipmaker Micron reported record quarterly revenue of $54.23 billion and $37.70 billion in profit. The reason: AI data centers need huge amounts of fast memory. It's one more sign of where the money in tech is going right now.
